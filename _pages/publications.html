How do mental health service systems and their workforce support recovery and equitable access to care?

My research examines how mental health service systems and the workforce that staffs them support recovery and equitable access to care. It moves across three connected areas, recovery-oriented practice and the peer workforce that delivers it, help-seeking and sustained engagement in services, and the growing role of technology in how care is sought and delivered.

Computational social science holds these areas together. I combine quantitative, computational, and mixed-methods approaches, including machine learning, natural language processing, structural topic modeling, causal inference, and evidence synthesis, to open sources of evidence that mental health services research has barely touched. This agenda is grounded in my frontline work with people living with serious mental illness in a clubhouse and a psychiatric hospital and is carried out with community partners.

Three Connected Areas

01

Recovery-Oriented Practice & the Peer Workforce

How practitioners translate recovery values into everyday care, and how systems define, integrate, and compensate the peer specialists who carry those values. I treat recovery-oriented care as structurally produced, shaped by organizational and community conditions rather than individual effort alone.

02

Help-Seeking & Sustained Engagement

How language, stigma, and social context shape who reaches mental health services and who stays. This work spans language-concordant care for Asian Americans, help-seeking among people with serious mental illness and East Asian students, and early dropout from treatment.

03

Technology & Mental Health Care

How digital and AI tools are changing the way care is sought and delivered, and where the human relationship remains essential. I study digital and AI-enabled help-seeking, peer support, and blended models that aim to extend care without weakening the therapeutic relationship.
